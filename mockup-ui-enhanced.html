Chapter 5: Cellular Respiration
Gemini 2.0 Flash
A
Can you explain the difference between aerobic and anaerobic respiration? I'm having trouble understanding when cells use each pathway.
✦

Great question! The key difference lies in oxygen availability and ATP yield:

Aerobic respiration uses oxygen and produces ~36–38 ATP per glucose molecule. It runs through glycolysis → pyruvate oxidation → Krebs cycle → electron transport chain.

Anaerobic respiration works without oxygen, producing only 2 ATP per glucose. Cells use this when oxygen is limited — like during intense exercise or in bacteria.

Think of aerobic as a slow-burning, highly efficient furnace, and anaerobic as a quick-burst generator.

A
What exactly happens in the Krebs cycle? Can you walk me through it step by step?
✦

The Krebs cycle (also called the citric acid cycle) occurs in the mitochondrial matrix. Here's the overview:

1. Acetyl-CoA (2C) combines with oxaloacetate (4C) → citrate (6C)
2. Citrate is rearranged → isocitrate
3. Isocitrate oxidized → α-ketoglutarate + CO₂ + NADH
4. α-ketoglutarate → succinyl-CoA + CO₂ + NADH
5. Succinyl-CoA → succinate + ATP (or GTP)
6. Succinate → fumarate + FADH₂
7. Fumarate → malate (water added)
8. Malate → oxaloacetate + NADH

Per turn: 3 NADH, 1 FADH₂, 1 ATP, 2 CO₂. The cycle runs twice per glucose molecule.

5.3 Cellular Respiration: Harvesting Chemical Energy
Cellular respiration can be summarized as the oxidation of glucose to produce ATP, carbon dioxide, and water. This process is the primary means by which most living cells generate ATP for cellular work. The overall reaction can be written as:

C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂ → 6CO₂ + 6H₂O + Energy (ATP)

During this process, electrons are transferred from organic molecules to oxygen. The "fall" of electrons from organic molecules to oxygen releases energy that can be used to synthesize ATP.

Aerobic respiration occurs in three main stages: glycolysis, the citric acid cycle (Krebs cycle), and oxidative phosphorylation via the electron transport chain and chemiosmosis.
Figure 5.3 — The Krebs Cycle
[Diagram: Circular flow of Krebs cycle intermediates with electron carrier production at each step]

The citric acid cycle is a series of chemical reactions used by all aerobic organisms to release stored energy through the oxidation of acetyl-CoA derived from carbohydrates, fats, and proteins. The cycle produces reduced electron carriers (NADH and FADH₂) that feed into the electron transport chain.

How many ATP does the full aerobic respiration produce?
✦

Aerobic respiration produces approximately 36–38 ATP per glucose molecule:

• Glycolysis: 2 ATP
• Krebs cycle: 2 ATP
• Electron transport: 32–34 ATP

Question
What is the primary function of the mitochondria, and why is it called the "powerhouse of the cell"?
Click to reveal answer
Answer
The mitochondria generates ATP (adenosine triphosphate) — the cell's energy currency — through cellular respiration. It's called the "powerhouse" because it produces ~90% of a cell's energy through oxidative phosphorylation via the electron transport chain.
